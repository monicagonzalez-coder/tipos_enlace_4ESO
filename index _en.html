SHORT VERSION · ENGLISH
One phase · 15–25 minutes

Five unknown solid samples have arrived, labelled A, B, C, D and E. They are known to be sodium chloride (NaCl), magnesium oxide (MgO), calcium chloride (CaCl₂), copper (Cu) and sucrose (C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁), but nobody knows which label belongs to which substance.

Your mission: investigate the properties of each sample and decide whether its predominant bonding is ionic, covalent or metallic. Drag a sample to an instrument, check that it is loaded, and press Run test. The sample will disappear from the instrument, but the result will remain in your laboratory notebook.

Important: these are simulated qualitative data. Ionic compounds form extended lattices, not individual molecules; this is why we refer to “substances” and “bonding types”.

Key reasoning: ions cannot move freely within a solid ionic lattice, but dissolved ions can carry charge. Solid copper conducts through delocalized electrons. Sucrose dissolves as neutral molecules, so its solution does not conduct appreciably.
